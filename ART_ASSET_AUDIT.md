# EBB — Art Asset Audit (Stage 01)

Audit date: 2026-09-12. Project: Godot 4.7.2, `D:\Games\Claude game`.
Scope: every sprite, sheet, tile, prop, enemy, NPC, boss, weapon, projectile, effect, UI element, shader, lighting system, parallax layer, place where art is drawn in code, and every hardcoded visual assumption.

Nothing here is a redesign. This is the map of what exists and what the code depends on, so that Stages 02+ can change the look without breaking the game.

Verdict key used in the tables:
- **REDESIGN** — the asset stays (same slot, same purpose), the drawing is replaced from scratch.
- **REPLACE** — the asset's concept itself is wrong (e.g. a boss that is a scaled-up enemy) and needs a new asset, usually with new dimensions.
- **RETAIN** — keep as is (only technical/utility assets qualify).
- **PROCEDURAL** — should stay generated at runtime or by script, but the generator must follow STYLE.md.

All current art is generated by `tools/gen_sprites.py` and its milestone add-ons (`gen_sprites_m4.py`, `_m4b.py`, `_m5.py`, `_m6.py`) from text grids and a shared dictionary palette. Regenerating rewrites `assets/sprites/*.png` and `.shots/sprites.png` (with `--preview`).

## 10. Water and tide (current implementation, to be replaced in Stage 10)

- `TideSystem.level` 0..4 rises 0.0015/s plus 0.16 per room. Cell floods when `level > height`, deep when `level > height + 1`.
- `Room._refresh_water` rebuilds the water `TileMapLayer` every 1/8 level: DEEP tile for deep cells; FOAM/FOAM2 for shallow cells with at least one 4-neighbour dry floor cell; WATER/WATER2 otherwise. Phase flips every 0.55 s (2-frame animation).
- The whole water layer is `modulate (1,1,1,0.62)`, z 1, so it is drawn over decor and under characters. Deep water uses the same alpha, so "deep" is only a darker tile, not an opaque one.
- Doors: the brief mentions water-closed doors and water-open shortcuts; the current code has no visual for either (doors are barred/open only by room clear).
- No shoreline direction, no ripple sprites, no splash on entry, no reflections, no wet-edge on the floor above the waterline.
- Titles/hub use the same system with the tide tweened 0.62-0.78 (title) or fixed 0.6 (hub).

Verdict: **REPLACE** the tile-swap look with an overlay system (shader on the water layer or authored autotile edges + animated foam), keeping the runtime contract: it must be computed from the height map at runtime and work over any floor.

## 15. Hardcoded visual assumptions (the checklist for later stages)

1. `tiles.gd` enum order mirrors `gen_sprites.TILE_NAMES` (+ m4, m4b, m6 lists). Any tile added or reordered must update both.
2. Every 24 px sheet has its frame indices listed in its script; changing frame counts means editing those dictionaries (allowed by the brief).
3. Sprite anchor offsets: player sprite y -9, enemies y -8, gull y -8 with shadow y +8, deep maw y 0; props y -4; lighthouse y -14. New art must place feet at the same rows or the offsets must be retuned per sheet.
4. Hurtbox and collider sizes were tuned to the current 16 px tall bodies (player hurtbox 10x16 at y -6). A taller Tam needs the hurtbox re-checked.
5. Weapon sprites: left-edge pivot, drawn pointing right, size tied to hitbox reach (gaff 20 px long).
6. `props.png` cells are 16 px (`Prop._frame`); `icons.png` cells 24 px; `pickups.png` 8 px; `door.png` 32 px; FX cells 48 / 12 px.
7. The slash pivot is at (4, 24) inside the 48 px cell and the code compensates with `offset (20, 0)`.
8. Boss scenes carry `scale = (2, 2)` or `(2.2, 2.2)` on the root node; collision shapes are in the scaled space. Replacing boss art with native 48/72 px sheets means resetting the scale to 1 and rescaling shapes.
9. HUD coordinates are absolute for 640x360. The 8 px strip at the bottom of the screen (y 352-360) is outside the room.
10. Night tint is applied before lights; sprites are authored in "daylight" colours and rely on the tint. STYLE.md must decide whether art is authored pre-tinted (then the CanvasModulate goes near-white) or stays daylight-authored (then the palette must be checked under the tint). Recommendation is made in Stage 02.
11. Water alpha 0.62 is a single constant; deep water is not opaque.
12. Room decor and ground tint are seeded from the template name, so the same template always looks the same (good for keyframes).
13. The camera never moves; parallax in the classic sense is impossible, and the room border (`#` ring) is always fully visible on screen.
14. `Settings.flashes()` gates full-screen flashes; new effects must respect it. `Settings.shake()` scales shake.
15. Title, hub and run all call `Atmosphere.build` with size (640, 368); the hub and title show the hub template with the tide fixed.
16. `Tidemarks.FAMILY_COLOR` has four families; the brief's fifth family (Echo) has no colour, icon, or data yet.
17. Codex entries carry `color` from the family colour; enemy entries have no portrait slot.
18. `.shots/` holds the screenshot workflow outputs (title.png, hub.png, latest.png) and generator previews (sprites.png). Screenshot commands: title `src/title/title.tscn -- --screenshot --sim-save`; hub `src/hub/hub.tscn -- --screenshot --sim-save [--bram|--codex|--pause|...]`; run `src/run/run.tscn -- --screenshot --sim-save --wait=300 [--biome=1|2] [--boss]`.

## 16. Visual QA of the current build (what the screenshots show)

Reference images: `.shots/title.png`, `hub.png`, `latest.png` (Streets), `sim_run.png` and `run_whisper.png` (Flats), `sim_depth_13.png` (Streets flooded), `sim_boss.png` (Ebb), `hub_codex.png`, `sim_altar.png`, `hub_pause2.png`, `sim_results.png`, `hub_ending_break.png`.

1. **Water does not read as water.** The diagonal hatch looks like rain on glass; at 62 % alpha the Streets become a uniform blue field where floor, decor and the raised island all merge. Foam is a dotted rectangle outline on every flooded cell that touches ground, on all four sides, which reads as a selection marquee.
2. **The lantern over-exposes.** The centre of Tam's light clips the sand to cream-white and hides the floor texture; it is a fog of light, not a lantern. Warm light is used by too many sources to feel precious.
3. **Biomes are not distinct enough.** Flats = grey sand, Streets = blue-grey cobbles, Hall = blue-grey basalt. Under the night tint the Streets and the Hall are nearly the same image. Decor in the Streets is invisible at 1x.
4. **Silhouettes collapse.** Player, still citizen, deckhand, mooring, lamplighter and the merchant are the same 16 px chibi template. Shellback, anchor, ebb and swimmer are rounded blobs in a single colour each. Nothing reads by shape.
5. **Scaling breaks the pixel grid.** Elites at 1.25x, the Ebb at 2.2x, boss at 1.9x, rings up to 3x, beacon smoothly scaled, net randomly rotated.
6. **Outlines everywhere.** Every sprite has a full 1 px near-black outline, including tiny props, so the world is a field of dark contours with no lighting logic.
7. **Off-palette accents.** Saturated red crabs and health bar, purple lamplighters and Deep family, yellow corpses, red-brown ruin bricks, red/white lighthouse, cyan door glows. The palette has no shared foundation (it is a dictionary of one-off colours per module).
8. **UI is generic.** Navy panels with cyan borders, bracketed key hints, 5x7 text at mixed sizes. Nothing references ledgers, charts, iron or lantern hardware. The tide meter, the game's central instrument, is a 4 px bar.
9. **No depth.** Walls are a flat top texture; there is no edge, no floor shadow, no vertical face, and the room border is a plain ring. Rooms feel like a board, not a place.
10. **Title lacks composition.** The logo is small and flat above a menu, on top of the ordinary hub map.

These findings are inputs to Stages 02-05. None of them are fixed in this stage.
