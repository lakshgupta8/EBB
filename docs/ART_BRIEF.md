# EBB — Art Direction Brief

For the artist building the visual style guide. Everything in the game today is placeholder pixel art generated by script; the goal is a style guide we can use to remake every sprite, tile, prop, effect and UI element. Nothing about the current look is precious. The constraints in section 2 are.

---

## 1. The game in one paragraph

EBB is a top-down 2D action roguelite. Every night the sea around Marrow Rock, a lighthouse island off a cold northern coast, pulls back impossibly far and exposes Saltmere, a city that drowned in a single night three hundred years ago. You are Tam, the new Tidekeeper, apprentice to Maren, who walked into the ruins and did not come back. You carry the keeper's lantern down through three biomes, fighting what lives there and rescuing the "echoes" of the drowned, until the tide comes back and takes you. Then you wake at the lighthouse and the night begins again. Each night you get stronger, the story goes deeper, and the hub village visibly changes.

**The hook: the tide is the clock.** Water physically rises through every run. Dry floor becomes shallow water, then deep water. Flooding slows you, opens and closes paths, and turns some enemies dangerous. Being caught by the tide ends the run but keeps more of your loot, so slowness is a gamble, not a punishment.

Tone: melancholy but warm. Short, dry dialog. Celtic coastal fishing village rather than Greek myth. Think of the cadence of Hades with the weather of the Hebrides. Cold, wet, salt, iron, rope, lamplight. Nothing gothic-horror; the drowned are not monsters, they are people who were kept "safe" too well.

## 2. Hard constraints (the engine side)

These are fixed by the game as built and the style has to live inside them.

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Internal resolution | 640 x 360, integer-scaled (2x at 1280x720, 3x at 1080p) |
| Perspective | Top-down, slightly angled (roughly three-quarter, like Hades or Enter the Gungeon). Floors are flat planes; walls show a top face only |
| Tile size | 16 x 16 px |
| Character cell | 24 x 24 px for the player and every enemy (the Wreck and Bell bosses are the same sheets scaled up 2-3x in engine) |
| Facing | Sprites are drawn facing right and mirrored horizontally in engine. No left-facing frames. The player aims with the mouse in 360 degrees; the body flips, the weapon rotates separately |
| Animation | Sprite-sheet strips, one row, fixed cell size. Frame counts in section 9 |
| Lighting | The whole scene has a dark blue night tint and the player carries a soft round lantern glow (a 48px radial gradient scaled up). Flooded tiles are drawn by a water shader over the floor with animated edges. Art should assume it is seen through a cold night filter with one warm light source |
| Format | PNG with alpha, no anti-aliasing against transparency, one file per sheet. Aseprite source files welcome |
| Font | A 5x7 bitmap font at 8 px, used at 1x, 2x, 3x. A replacement bitmap font is in scope if the style wants one |

The screen must stay readable at 640x360 with ten enemies, water, foam, projectiles and the HUD on it. Readability beats detail everywhere.

## 3. The world

### The hub: Marrow Rock
A lighthouse on a rock shelf. Wet sand, a low stone wall, a few cottages implied off screen, rock pools, kelp, driftwood, shells. The tide line is visible at the bottom of the hub and the "descend" door sits in the water. Over the course of the game the hub grows: more lanterns lit, the rescued Drowned build a small dock, Maren's window is lit in one ending, the lower village floods in another. Six NPCs stand here (section 4).

### Biome 1: The Flats
Exposed tidal flats. Rippled sand, standing water in the low spots, shipwreck ribs, rope, nets, crab holes, gull-picked bones, kelp beds, barnacled rock walls. The most open and the most "natural" biome. Dawn-grey sand under a night sky, lantern light warm on it.

### Biome 2: The Drowned Streets
Saltmere's streets, freshly uncovered. Cobbles, collapsed brick and stone houses, lampposts that have not burned in three centuries, benches, market pots, doorways, moss and weed everywhere, a Celtic fishing town preserved underwater. The Still Citizens stand in the streets exactly where they were the night it drowned. This biome floods fastest and most of it is shallow water by the end. Muted stone, verdigris, salt-white crust, dark green weed.

### Biome 3: The Deep Hall
The cathedral of the Ebb at the bottom. Basalt, tall pews, chains, dead lamps, candle drifts that still burn, channels cut in the floor that fill first, a raised dais. Every previous Tidekeeper stands here like a statue, still holding a lantern. Darker and more vertical in feeling than the other two. Black rock, cold blue water, small warm points of candle light.

### Biome 1.5 (planned expansion): The Sunken Harbour
Between the Flats and the Streets. Where Saltmere's ships tried to leave. Hulls, chains, cranes, a drydock that floods in stages. Not built yet; the style guide should leave room for it.

### The tide, visually
Every room has a height map of four bands (0 to 3). As the tide level rises, lower bands go under first. The artist needs a clear visual language for:
- dry floor per biome
- low ground (floods first) versus raised ground (floods last)
- shallow water (walkable, slows you) versus deep water (hurts you, some enemies hunt you in it)
- the foam edge where water meets floor
- doors that are shut by water and shortcut doors that only open when flooded
- the challenge room: no doors, tide rising, survive

Currently the water is a blue diagonal-hatch overlay with a dotted white edge. The remake can replace this entirely, but it must stay a tint or overlay that works over any floor tile because the flood is computed at runtime, not painted.

## 4. The cast

### Tam (the player)
Silent-ish, gender never specified, young. A hooded oilskin coat, boots, the keeper's lantern on a hip or hand. Reads as a working fisher, not a hero. The lantern is the tether that brings you back when the tide takes you, and it is warm to the touch in a way lanterns should not be; it should look ordinary but slightly wrong. Animations: idle, walk, hurt, dash, plus a death fall. Weapons are separate sprites rotated around Tam toward the mouse.

### Hub NPCs
| NPC | Who they are | Notes for design |
|---|---|---|
| Elder Orla | Village elder. Blunt, guilty. Sells permanent upgrades from "the Ledger" | Old, stern, wrapped against the wind. The only one who knew what the keeper title meant |
| Fen | Village kid who thinks you are a hero | Small, bright, the one warm colour in the hub |
| Bram the Drowned | The first echo you rescue. A Saltmere blacksmith. Runs the forge, weapons | An echo: a person three hundred years drowned, preserved not rotted. Wet, pale, calm. Big, kind |
| Sister Wren | Echo, Saltmere priestess. Blesses and bans boons, keeps the codex | Robes, a bell or a book, serene |
| The Cartographer | Echo, nameless. Sells map fragments | Thin, ink-stained, face never fully seen |
| Maren | The old keeper. Found at the bottom, mid-bargain. Gives the endings | Should share a silhouette with Tam: same coat, same lantern, older. She is what Tam becomes |

The echoes need a consistent "drowned" treatment that reads as sad and gentle, not undead: pale, damp, a faint cold glow, maybe a strand of weed, never rot or bone.

### Enemies (all 24 x 24 cells, seven-frame strips)
Every enemy telegraphs every attack with a visible windup, and the engine flashes them white and plays a ring right before they strike. Silhouettes must be distinct at a glance in water at 1x.

**Biome 1, The Flats**
- Shellback: slow tank. A great barnacled shell on legs. Slams. Cracks open when hit by a charged attack
- Gull: dive-bomber. Flies over water, swoops
- Wrack Crab: charger. Red, claws up, flattens when stunned
- Tidewisp (design only, not yet built): ranged, flees

**Biome 2, The Drowned Streets**
- Still Citizen: a statue of a townsperson until you are close, then fast. Mossy, arms down, then arms up
- Lamplighter: carries a lit pole, buffs the others, throws light
- Bell Ringer: small hunched figure with a hand bell, summons more
- Swimmer: exists only in flooded tiles. A drowned person swimming fast, jaws and ripples

**Biome 3, The Deep Hall**
- Keeper's Shade: a dark echo of a Tidekeeper, mirrors your weapon. Same silhouette as Tam, black and blue
- Anchor: a heavy iron thing that pulls you toward water on a chain
- Deep Maw: a mouth in the floor that opens

**Elites** (currently tinted versions of normal enemies; unique art wanted)
- The Netted (Flats): a drowned fisherman who throws nets
- The Choir (Streets): a group that sings, later joins the hub
- Echo of Maren (Hall)

**Bosses**
- The Wreck (Flats): a ship that fights. Currently a scaled-up Shellback; needs its own design. Hull, ribs, chains, a figurehead
- The Bell of Saltmere (Streets): the town bell. Rings to summon, phases with water level. Currently a scaled-up Bell Ringer
- The Ebb (Hall, final): the thing beneath the sea that agreed to keep Saltmere safe. Currently a blue hooded figure with a lantern. Should not be a monster; it holds people still because it was asked to. Phases change as the water rises around it

## 5. Weapons, boons and effects

### Weapons (small sprites rotated around the player)
| Weapon | Feel |
|---|---|
| Gaff Hook | Default. A fisher's long hook. Three-hit combo, third hit pulls enemies in. Hold to charge a heavy sweep that cracks shells. Throw and yank yourself to it |
| Net and Knife | Fast stabs, thrown net roots enemies (the net is a 24px sprite that lands on the floor) |
| Harpoon Gun | Slow piercing bolts, three then a reload. Tethered harpoon drags an enemy along your dashes |
| Storm Lantern | Wide arcs of light, low damage. Enemies hit are "lit" and take bonus damage. Beacon: a pool of light on the floor |
| Keeper's Bell, Line and Sinker | Planned |

### Tidemarks (per-run boons) come in five families; the UI colours them and the style guide should give each an icon language
- Salt (offense): white crust, crystal
- Brine (defense): dark green, kelp, shell
- Current (mobility): pale blue, wake, foam
- Deep (weird): near-black blue, pressure, bubbles
- Echo (synergy, unlocked by rescued NPCs): pale gold, lantern light

Three rarities.

### Effects that exist now and need a matching style
- melee slash arc (4 frames, 48px), hit spark, expanding ring for the flare and riptide, lantern glow, drop shadow
- hit flash (white), enemy windup flash, "lit" status glow, bleed, root (netted), stun
- water ripples, foam, splash when entering water, bubbles in deep water
- lantern whisper text that appears over the scene at story moments

### Pickups and currencies
- Salt (run currency, dropped by enemies): small white crystal
- Pearls (permanent currency): small pearl
- Echo shards (rare, story)
- Chests (closed, open), cages holding echoes (closed, open), fountains (heal), altars (three boon choices)

## 6. UI

Everything is in the same pixel grid, drawn at 1x and scaled. Current UI is white 5x7 text on dark navy panels with thin light borders.

- HUD: health bar, dash charges (three pips), weapon special bar, flare meter, harpoon ammo, salt and pearl counters, the vertical tide meter on the right edge, room counter and biome name bottom left, boss health bar bottom centre
- Door icons (24px) shown on doors before you choose a room: combat, elite, fountain, treasure, boss, shop, altar, echo cage
- Altar choice (three cards), shop, results screen, pause menu, settings, codex (enemies, tidemarks, people, places), Ledger (upgrade nodes), ending screens, dialog lines with NPC name
- Title screen with the lighthouse and the EBB logo (currently 88x30 px)
- Mouse cursor and gamepad button prompts

---

## 7. What we want from the style guide

1. A palette. The world is night, cold and wet with one warm light. We want a limited, deliberate palette that gives each biome its own cast while sharing a base, and that keeps the water readable over every floor.
2. Rules: outline or no outline, shading direction, how much detail per 16px tile, how water and light are drawn, proportions of characters in a 24px cell (currently about 16px tall, big head, small body).
3. One keyframe mock per biome plus the hub at 640x360, with the HUD on it, at least one flooded. This is the reference everything else gets checked against.
4. Character sheet: Tam turnaround, Tam and Maren side by side, the drowned treatment applied to one echo, one enemy per biome, one boss.
5. A tile study: the four height bands dry, shallow and deep for one biome, with the foam edge and a door.
6. A UI kit: panel, button, bar, icon style, the five tidemark family marks, the door icons, and a recommendation on the font.
7. Logo and title treatment.

Anything about the current look you would throw away, throw away. The proportions, tile size, resolution and sheet layouts are the only things the code depends on, and even frame counts can grow if you want more animation.

---

## 8. Optional touchstones

Not a style to copy, just the register: coastal Britain and Ireland in winter, oilskins and rope, wet slate, gas lamps; the tone of "The Lighthouse" without the madness; Moonlighter and Hades for readability at speed; Dredge for the sea-that-is-wrong feeling.
