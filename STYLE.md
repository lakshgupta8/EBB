# EBB — Visual Style Bible (STYLE.md)

Version 1.8, Stage 10, 2026-09-20 (first written 1.0, Stage 02, 2026-09-12). This is the hard specification for every future asset. When an asset and this document disagree, the asset is wrong. Changes to this document are made deliberately, dated, and listed at the end.

Target in one sentence: **small, expressive, atmospheric pixel art with exceptional silhouette and lighting discipline** — a cold, wet, quiet northern shore at night, with one warm light in the player's hand.

Tone in three words: **melancholy, warm, uncanny.** Not horror. Not gothic. Not cartoon.

---

## 0. The ten laws (read these even if you read nothing else)

1. **One art pixel is one screen pixel at 640x360.** No sub-pixel art, no smooth scaling, no rotation of pixel art except in 90-degree steps and the two engine-sanctioned exceptions (weapons around Tam, projectiles along their path).
2. **Author art pre-tinted for night.** Sprites and tiles are painted in the night palette. The engine's night `CanvasModulate` becomes nearly neutral. Lights add; they no longer have to fight a heavy blue multiply.
3. **Warm light is precious.** Warm palette colours appear only on light sources and what they touch. On any screen the warm sources are: Tam's lantern, plus at most three others.
4. **Silhouette first.** Every gameplay entity must be identifiable as a black shape at 1x. If it is not, the design is not finished.
5. **Outlines are for gameplay entities.** Characters, enemies, bosses, projectiles and pickups get a 1 px Ink outline. Tiles never do. Props get a grounding edge only. Effects and UI have their own rules.
6. **Clusters, not noise.** Colour changes come in shapes of at least 2x2 pixels except for deliberate 1 px accents (eyes, highlights, salt, rain). No random speckle.
7. **Light comes from above.** Ambient key light is top-down and cold. Top faces are lighter, bottom edges darker. The lantern's warmth is added by the engine, never painted into a sprite (exceptions: the lantern itself and static flames).
8. **Detail density follows importance.** Player and bosses highest, enemies and interactables medium, floor and background lowest. The floor must never be busier than what stands on it.
9. **Every biome has its own cast, on a shared foundation.** Same ramps, different mixes. If a screenshot cannot be placed in a biome within a second, the tile set is wrong.
10. **Readability beats detail.** When a choice trades beauty for legibility at ten enemies plus water plus HUD, legibility wins.

## 1. Pixel density and scale

| Thing | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Screen | 640x360 | Integer scaled to the window. Art is judged at 1x and 2x. |
| Tile | 16x16 | Floors, walls, water overlay cells. |
| Character cell | 24x24 | Player, enemies, NPCs (NPCs move from 16 px to 24 px cells). |
| Boss cell | 48x48 or 72x72 | Native sheets. Node scale is always 1. Hitboxes re-tuned to the art. |
| Major prop | 16x24, 24x24, 32x32, 32x48 | Fountain, altar, cage, chest, statue, lighthouse. Authored at the size the object needs; anchored at the bottom centre. |
| Decor | 8x8, 16x16, 16x8 | Small scatter. Never larger than a character's torso unless it is a major prop. |
| Pickup | 8x8 | |
| Projectile | 8x8 to 16x8 | |
| Effect | 16, 24, 32, 48 cells | Authored at the size it plays at. No effect is scaled by more than 1x. |
| UI | 1x pixel grid | Nine-slice panels, 8 px icon grid, 16 px large icons. |
| Fonts | 8 px base | Sizes 8, 16, 24 only. |

Forbidden: node `scale` on any sprite other than exactly 1 or -1 (flip). The existing 1.25 elite, 1.9/2.0/2.2 boss, 2.6 ring and beacon scaling are removed as those assets are rebuilt.

## 2. Pixel clusters

> Drawn examples for sections 2-5: `assets/reference/pixel_rules.png` (generated by `tools/gen_pixel_rules.py`, every example at 1x and 4x, DO in green, DON'T in red). Panel letters are cited below. When a rule and the sheet disagree, fix the sheet; the text is the rule.

- **Minimum cluster:** 2x2 for any colour region inside a sprite or tile. A 1 px cluster is allowed only as a *named accent*: eye, catch-light, salt grain, rain drop, ember, rivet, rope knot.
- **Texture is drawn, not sprinkled.** A stone tile has 3 to 5 drawn stones, not 40 random pixels. A sand tile has 2 or 3 ripple lines, not noise.
- **Curves** follow standard pixel curve steps (1-2-3 stepping, no lone corner pixels). Circles use the 8-fold symmetric templates; ovals stretch the same steps.
- **Diagonals** use consistent runs (1:1, 2:1, 3:1). Never mix 2:1 and 3:1 on the same edge.
- **Dithering** is permitted in exactly two places: the water overlay (2-colour checker at the shallow-to-deep transition) and large soft shapes in effects (mist edge, lantern pool edge). Never on characters, props or UI.
- **Anti-aliasing:** none against transparency. Internal anti-aliasing only between two adjacent ramp colours and only on curves of large shapes (bosses, major props, logo).

Sheet: panel G (clusters vs noise), H (the two dithers, and dither-as-shading forbidden), I (circle template, diagonal runs), J (no anti-aliasing against alpha).

## 3. Outline rules

| Category | Outline | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Player, enemies, bosses, NPCs | Full 1 px outline around the silhouette | **Ink** `#10121c` |
| Projectiles, pickups | Full 1 px outline | Ink |
| Weapons (held) | Full 1 px outline | Ink |
| Major props (fountain, altar, cage, chest, statue, lighthouse) | Outline on the bottom and shadow-side edges only (a "grounding edge"); the top and lit edges are open | Darkest ramp colour of the material, not Ink |
| Decor (shell, rope, kelp, planks) | No outline; the object is a colour shape on the floor with a 1 px darker underside where it has height | — |
| Tiles (floor, wall tops, water) | No outline ever | — |
| Wall faces | 1 px Ink line where the wall meets the floor (this is the wall's cast shadow, see 4) | Ink |
| Effects | No outline. Two-tone shapes, occasionally a 1 px darker core | — |
| UI panels | 1 px Iron light edge outside, 1 px Ink inside (a bevel) | see 15 |
| Logo, title | 1 px Ink outline, plus a 1 px offset drop shadow | see 16 |

Internal lines inside a character (coat opening, belt, hood edge) use the **darkest colour of that material's ramp**, never Ink. Ink appears inside a character only for the eye and the mouth line on drowned faces.

Selective outline break: on characters the outline may open for a single pixel at the very top of a rounded lit form (hood crown, shell top) to let the highlight touch the edge. Use this sparingly; it is a polish move, not a rule.

Sheet: panel A (the 24 px figure with Ink outside and ramp-dark inside, against the two wrong versions), C (wall foot line, no tile outlines), D (prop grounding edge against a fully inked prop that reads as an enemy).

## 4. Shadow rules

**Key light** is top-down and slightly from the top-left (the sky). Every form is shaded as a cylinder lit from above: top face lightest, vertical faces mid, undersides darkest.

**Ramp discipline:** a material gets a 3-step ramp (dark, mid, light) plus an optional highlight. Shadow is the dark step, never black, never a different hue unless the material's ramp says so (see 6).

**Drop shadows (entities):** a separate blob sprite under every grounded entity, `Ink` at 45 % alpha, width = 60 % of the body width, height 4 px (6 px for bosses). Flying things (gull) keep a shadow on the floor offset by their height. Water-borne things (swimmer) have no shadow; they have a ripple.

**Cast shadow (environment):** every wall casts a 3 px shadow band on the floor along its south edge (the side facing the camera), drawn as a floor overlay tile, `Ink` at 35 % alpha. West and east edges get a 1 px band. This is what makes walls read as height. Rocks and major props get a 2 px band on the south side inside their own sprite.

**Under-water:** entities standing in shallow water keep a faint shadow (25 %). In deep water, none.

No painted lantern shadows. No long cast shadows from characters.

Sheet: panel B (the 45 % blob and the floating figure without it), C (3 px south band, 1 px west band, Ink foot), E (key light on a cube, a cylinder and a hooded head).

## 5. Highlight rules

- Each material ramp has at most **one** highlight colour, used only where the material justifies it (see 6).
- Highlights are **placed**, never scattered: the top edge of a rounded form, the rim of a wet surface, a single catch-light in an eye, the lip of glass.
- Highlight size: 1 px wide runs of 1 to 4 px on characters; up to 6 px on bosses and major props; never a filled region.
- **Wet highlight** is the signature accent of the world: a 1 px run of **Salt white** `#d9e3e8` on the top edge of anything wet (oilskin shoulders, wet stone tops, shells, drowned heads). Dry things (rope, wood interiors, wool) never get Salt white.
- Metal highlight uses the ramp's light step, not white. Only glass, wet surfaces, and eyes may use Salt white or Foam white.
- Warm highlights (Warm yellow, Pale cream) appear **only** on light sources and their glass, and on the 2-3 px of surface immediately around a flame.

Sheet: panel F (one Salt white run on wet stone; sprinkled highlights, Salt white on dry rope, and a second highlight set on a figure all shown as DON'T).

## 7. Character proportions

Cell is 24x24. Feet rest on **row 21** (0-indexed), leaving rows 22-23 empty for the separate shadow sprite to sit under the feet without the body drifting. The sprite node offset stays y = -9 for the player (engine fact) and is re-tuned once per sheet.

**Tam (the reference human)** — 20 px tall, roughly **3 heads**:

```
row  0 -  1   empty (headroom for hood tips, hats, bell yokes)
row  2 -  8   head + hood        7 px tall, 7 px wide (face 4 px wide, 3 px visible under the hood)
row  9 - 16   torso + coat       8 px tall, 8-9 px wide at the shoulder, coat flares to 10 px at the hem
row 17 - 21   legs + boots       5 px tall, boots 3 px each with a 1 px gap
row 22 - 23   reserved for the shadow sprite
```

- Head is one third of the height. Not the half-height chibi head of the placeholder.
- Arms are 2 px wide and read as separate shapes from the torso (1 px gap or a darker line).
- Hands: 2x2 px in Skin. The lantern hand is always the hand furthest from the aim when possible; the lantern hangs from the hip on the off side otherwise (a fixed 5x7 lantern sprite inside the body frame).
- Face: two 1 px Ink eyes with no white, a 1 px Skin shade mouth line only on hurt frames. No noses.
- Hood up by default. Hood interior is Oilskin dark, so the face sits in shadow.

**Human enemies and NPCs** use the same skeleton with costume differences and posture differences:
- Still Citizen: same height, arms pinned, shoulders square, feet together.
- Lamplighter: 21 px (taller hat), pole to row 0.
- Bell Ringer: 16 px tall, hunched, 11 px wide.
- Maren: Tam's exact skeleton, 1 px more stoop, hair out of the hood.
- Orla: 18 px, wrapped shawl widens her to 11 px.
- Fen: 14 px tall, head 6 px (kids are 2.3 heads tall).

**Non-human enemies** fill the cell by mass, not height: Shellback 18x12 low; Wrack Crab 20x10 low and wide; Gull 16x12 with a 20 px wingspan on the spread frame; Swimmer 22x8; Anchor 16x18 (iron mass on a hunched body); Deep Maw 22x14 flat.

**Bosses:** The Wreck 72x48 wide (a hull on its side); The Bell of Saltmere 48x72 tall; The Ebb 72x72. Their mass must be at least four times a normal enemy's, and their silhouette must still be a single recognisable shape when reduced to 24 px (the codex icon test).

## 8. Environment detail density

Three tiers. An asset's tier decides how many colours, how many clusters, and whether it may have 1 px accents.

| Tier | What | Colours per asset | Accents | Contrast vs floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Player, bosses, named NPCs, interactables (altar, cage, chest, fountain, doors) | 6-9 | yes | Highest. Full outline or strong grounding edge. |
| **Medium** | Normal enemies, elites, major props, weapons, projectiles | 5-7 | few | High. Full outline. |
| **Low** | Decor, floor tiles, wall tops, water, far scenery | 3-4 | none, or 1 px salt only | Low. Floor tiles stay within a narrow value band (see 20). |

Floor tiles per biome use at most **4 colours including the base**, and the base covers at least 60 % of the tile. Wall tops may use 4. Water overlay 3 plus foam.

Decor density per room: 10-18 pieces, placed by rules (never in a doorway, never on raised stone unless the biome says so, kelp only on low ground or beside water, benches and pots only in the Streets, candles only in the Hall). Room type modifies density: fountain and altar rooms are sparse, treasure rooms are cluttered on one side.

### Where the palette lives
- `tools/palette.py` is the machine copy: the table above, biome subsets (10), family colours, UI text roles, and the luminance/contrast report (`python tools/palette.py --check`). Every sprite generator imports its colours from here.
- `src/core/palette.gd` (`Palette.INK`, `Palette.FAMILY`, `Palette.TEXT`, `Palette.BIOME`, ...) is **generated** by `python tools/palette.py`. Never edit it by hand; never write a colour literal in engine code when a palette name exists.
- `assets/reference/palette.png` is the eyeball copy: swatches with luminance, the luminance ladder with each biome's floor band, biome strips with a Tam mock, the night-tint check and the UI contrast check. Regenerated by the same command.

### Engine colours
The night `CanvasModulate` becomes `Palette.NIGHT_TINT_RUN` `(0.92, 0.95, 1.0)` in runs and `Palette.NIGHT_TINT_HUB` `(0.96, 0.97, 1.0)` in the hub and title (switched in Stage 11, when the art is pre-tinted). The clear colour is Abyss (done). Tidemark family colours (`Palette.FAMILY`, used by `tidemarks.gd`): Salt = Salt red, Brine = Verdigris, Current = Sea pale, Deep = Deep violet, Echo = Amber; each family icon carries a light mark (`Palette.FAMILY_MARK`): Salt white, Salt white, Foam white, Violet light, Pale cream. Particle colours must be picked from this table.

### What the contrast check says (and the rules it forces)
- Salt white, Pale slate, Warm yellow, Verdigris, Sea pale and Amber all pass 4.5:1 on the panel face. **Salt red (2.8:1) and Deep violet (1.8:1) do not**, and Wet grey is 2.8:1 by design (muted). So: family colours and Salt red never carry 8 px text (see 15).
- On the hover face (Slate) only Salt white and Warm yellow pass. Hovered rows show all text as primary.
- The Ink outline is below 3:1 against the Hall floor (1.5:1) and the Harbour deck (1.7:1). Those floors stay that dark on purpose; readability there comes from the entity's body colours, not its outline (rule 11 in 20).

## 11. Lighting

**Model:** cold ambient night + additive local light + subtle wet reflections.

- Art is pre-tinted, so the ambient is *in the paint*. The `CanvasModulate` is near-neutral (see 9) and exists only to pull the whole frame a hair cooler and to let the ending sequences fade.
- `PointLight2D` is additive. Because the paint is already dark, lights need far less energy than today. Target: Tam's lantern energy ~0.7, colour Warm yellow at 60 % mix toward Pale cream, texture scale ~3.2 (a 150 px pool with a 60 px bright core), breathing ±8 %. The floor under Tam must **never** exceed Bone light in value; sand texture stays visible inside the pool.
- **Light texture:** replace the Gaussian `glow.png` with a two-zone falloff: flat 40 % core, then a steep fall to zero. Pools read as lantern light (a lit circle with an edge), not as fog. A second, smaller texture (24 px) serves candles, eyes and pickups.
- **Warm sources** (all use the same three-colour language: Amber edge, Warm yellow body, Pale cream core; flame sprite 2 frames): Tam's lantern; the lighthouse lamp (a beam texture, not a giant pool); candles in the Hall; lamplighter poles; the Storm Lantern beacon; the "lit" status (small, Amber, on the enemy's body); hub windows and Fen's lamp. Budget: Tam plus three.
- **Cold sources** use Sea pale at low energy (0.3-0.5) and small scale (1.2-2.0): open doors, fountains, altars, cages, the Ebb, Keeper's Shade eyes, drowned echoes at rest. Cold light says "the sea is here"; it must never look like cyan neon. No cold source is brighter than Tam's lantern.
- **Reflections:** on water cells within a light's radius, the water shader adds 1 px horizontal glints of the light's colour at 30 % (Stage 10). No painted reflections.
- **Bloom:** none. Screen flashes stay (flare, rescue) at reduced alpha (max 0.4) and short (0.25 s).
- **Room edges:** beyond the walls is Abyss with a very faint Night 1 texture; light does not reach it.

## 12. Water

The tide is the game. Its four states must be readable without the HUD:

| State | Floor visible? | Overlay | Edge | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dry** | yes | none | — | none |
| **Low ground (will flood first)** | yes, darker | none; the tile itself is wet: Sand dark / Moss / Deep sea channel with 1 px Salt white grains | a 1 px Salt white "tide line" where low meets normal floor (autotile edge) | none |

Additional water language:
- **Splash** (entering water, dashing in water): 16x16, 3 frames, Salt white and Foam white droplets, no outline.
- **Ripple ring** (standing/walking in shallow water, swimmers): 16x8 ellipse, 3 frames, 1 px Shallow, fades.
- **Bubbles** (deep water, drowned things rising): 4x4, 2 frames, Sea pale ring.
- **Wet edge on entities:** anything standing in shallow water is drawn with a 2 px Sea-tinted band across its feet (shader or a small overlay sprite), so wading reads.
- **Doors and water:** a *flooded* doorway shows water inside the arch and foam on its sill; a *water-closed* door has an iron grate lowered with the water line across it; a *water-opened shortcut* is a low arch that is dark and barred while dry and becomes an open Sea-lit passage once flooded (authored as three door states, Stage 10).
- The overlay is computed at runtime from the height map, so all water art is transparent PNG or shader, never painted into floor tiles.
- Water reflects lights (see 11) and nothing else.
- **As built (Stage 10, change log 1.8):** `assets/sprites/water.png` holds the bodies, the 16-case foam set, the wet band and the tide line; `room.gd` picks every cell from the height map and the tide (`Room.Body`, `_foam_case`, the Edge layer). Foam faces dry floor only, never a wall. The water layers draw *under* entities; wading reads on the body (wet edge, splash, ripple, faint shadow). Deep water is dithered two tiles in from anything shallow, and goes to Abyss five tiles in. The tide line is dashed (4 on, 2 off) at full Salt white; the wet band is Ink at 35 %. Doors: six arches, see 1.8.

## 13. Fog and atmosphere

- No full-screen fog and no vignette. Darkness is made with paint, not with overlays.
- **Salt haze:** two or three large soft sprites (128 px, 3-colour dither edge) in Salt white at 4-6 % alpha, drifting 6-10 px per minute, only in the Flats and the hub. The Streets have none (enclosed). The Hall has a single, lower, colder haze in Sea at 4 %.
- **Spray:** replace the drifting white dots with a sparse emitter along water edges only: 1 px Salt white specks at 25 % alpha, short lives (1.5 s), rising 6 px and fading. Rate scaled by the flooded area.
- **Drips** (Streets, Hall): occasional 1 px Sea pale drops falling 6 px from wall tops, 1 per 4 s per room.
- **Room darkness:** floors never go below Basalt dark in value; the player must always see the walkable area. Mood comes from the palette, not from black.

- Base 8 px bitmap. **Only integer sizes: 8, 16, 24.** The current sizes 12, 14 and 20 are retired (banner, room label and ending title map to 16; "boss stirs" to 16; the biome banner at run start to 24).

## 18. Drowned-person treatment

The drowned are people kept too safe. They are never monsters.

**Always:**
- Skin in the Drowned ramp (`#7e9694` / `#b7c8c3`); a 1 px Salt white wet highlight on the crown or brow.
- Eyes: 1 px Ink, calm, slightly lowered; on echoes at rest a 1 px Sea pale catch-light (the only thing that glows).
- Posture: still, arms down, weight even, shoulders slightly forward. Movement is deliberate; drowned things do not flail.
- Clothes: the same cut as living villagers, desaturated toward the Slate ramp (a fisher's coat in Slate/Night 2 instead of Wool).
- One strand of kelp or weed (Kelp, 1-2 px wide, 3-6 px long) at the shoulder, hem or hair. Never more than two.
- A faint cold rim: a 1 px Sea pale line along the top of the shoulders and head (this replaces any in-engine glow on normal enemies; echoes and the merchant may also have a small cold `PointLight2D` at 0.3).
- Water language nearby: a 2-frame drip, a ripple when standing in water.
- Still Citizens are the same people crusted with Moss on their lower half and salt on their shoulders (Salt white 1 px runs); when they wake, the crust frames stay on the body (they carry their stillness with them), only the eyes open (catch-light appears) and the arms rise.
- Swimmers are drowned people swimming: a pale head and shoulders above the water line, arms in a stroke, the body under the water drawn in Sea (the overlay hides it). Not a fish. Jaws are a wide calm mouth, not fangs.

**Never:** exposed bone, rot, wounds, blood, torn flesh, sunken zombie eyes, green slime, fangs, claws on humans, screaming mouths.

**Learned at Stage 06 (the first Citizen read as a zombie):** the corpse read comes from three things and each is banned: (1) Drowned shade under the eyes or on the cheeks (sunken sockets): shade goes under the chin and brow only; (2) a bare pale head: every drowned person has hair, a beard, a scarf or a hat framing the face in the Wool or Slate ramps, so the pale skin is a face, not a skull; (3) a pale mass of skin: Drowned skin appears only on the face (4-5 px wide) and hands. Give them a gesture that says waiting, not lurching: hands clasped in front, a head a pixel bowed. Moss on a Still Citizen is a low band at the hem and boots, never on the torso or face.

**Emotional read:** sadness, gentleness, patience. A drowned NPC at the hub should look like someone waiting for a boat.

## 20. Readability rules

1. **Contrast ladder** (highest to lowest): Tam > bosses > enemies > interactables and doors > water edges > major props > decor > floor. If a lower rung out-contrasts a higher one in a screenshot, the lower one is toned down, never the higher one raised.
2. **Floor value band:** all floor tiles in a biome stay within a 15 % luminance band, so anything standing on them pops. Walls are one band darker (their tops) with an Ink line at the foot.
3. **Outlines make the ladder work:** only gameplay entities have Ink outlines (see 3), so a 1x squint shows exactly the things that can hurt or help you.
4. **Water must be readable over any floor:** the shallow overlay is checked over Sand, Stone, Basalt and Wood; foam is always the shore's brightest element; deep water is the only place the floor disappears.
5. **Telegraphs:** windup frames hold a distinct pose; the engine's Salt white flash and Salt red ring stay. Red is reserved for danger and the crab's claws.
6. **Effects are transparent to gameplay:** max 3 frames of an enemy hidden; no effect brighter than Foam white; no effect larger than the flare.
7. **Warm means important:** warm colours mark Tam, help (fountains no, they are cold; the beacon yes, hub friends yes), and the flare. An enemy carrying warm light (lamplighter) is a priority target by design.
8. **Ten-enemy test:** every enemy design is checked in a mock with ten enemies, water, foam, projectiles and the HUD at 1x. If two enemy types cannot be told apart in that mock, one is redesigned.
9. **Text over the world** always has the 1 px Ink shadow; banners get a 2 px shadow at 16 px.
10. **The player must always be findable in under half a second.** Tam's outline, the lantern pool and the warm lantern colour are the three guarantees; none of them are ever given to an enemy at full strength (the Keeper's Shade has a cold lantern, the Lamplighter's light is smaller and on a pole).
11. **Body colour carries the dark biomes.** The Ink outline is under 3:1 against the Hall and Harbour floors by design (they are near-black). Every gameplay entity therefore carries at least one body colour that reads 3:1 or better against every floor it can stand on (Drowned skin, Bone, Iron light, Sea pale and Salt white all qualify against Basalt); a design whose only contrast is its outline is redrawn.
